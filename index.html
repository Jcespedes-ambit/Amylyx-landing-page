About the Condition

About Non-Cystic Fibrosis Bronchiectasis (NCFB)

Bronchiectasis is a long-term lung condition in which the bronchial tubes (the airways) become scarred and widened. Mucus pools in the damaged areas, which makes the lungs more prone to infection. Recurrent infections can set off a vicious cycle that causes more injury to the airways and the surrounding lung tissue.

"Non-cystic fibrosis" means this form is not caused by cystic fibrosis. In more than half of cases the underlying cause is never found. When there is one, it is usually acquired rather than inherited — the airways were damaged by something such as a past chest infection (like whooping cough or pneumonia), an inhaled object, aspiration, or lowered immunity after an infection.

It can begin at any age, though it often starts in childhood, and the symptoms can look like other common chest problems — so bronchiectasis is sometimes overlooked. Speaking with an Ambit Guide is a simple first step toward understanding your (or a loved one's) symptoms and the support that may be available.

Signs people often notice

  • A chronic wet cough — coughing up mucus, lasting more than four weeks
  • Wheeze or shortness of breath, especially during exercise
  • Chest infections that keep coming back
  • Weight loss or poor weight gain
  • Finger or toe clubbing (fuller soft tissue at the fingertips or toes)

Symptoms vary from person to person. Educational information only — not a diagnosis.

The Facts About NCFB

Plain-language context to help you understand the broader picture.

4+ weeks

A hallmark sign is a wet cough that lasts longer than four weeks and shouldn't be dismissed.

>50%

In more than half of cases, the underlying cause is never identified.

Acquired

Non-CF bronchiectasis is acquired, not an inherited condition.

Lifelong

It's a chronic condition that often needs ongoing, lifelong management.

Source: Australian Paediatric Surveillance Unit (APSU), Non-Cystic Fibrosis Bronchiectasis — Information for Parents.
